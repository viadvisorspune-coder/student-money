03

Margin

Data visualisation

Charts for a small study: six participants, six diary days, three rounds of testing. With n = 6, every participant is visible as a mark; nothing is turned into a percentage that hides how few people it is. Charts inherit the page: paper ground, ink hairline axes, blue marks, one red mark the chart is about, and a note in the margin instead of a legend wherever possible.

All figures on this page are sample data

03.1

Data

Quantitative

Counts and measures from testing. Show the people, not the percentage: six marks per row, their shape telling the outcome.

Use
Unit charts whenever n is 12 or fewer. A line only for one person or one measure over time.

Never
Percentages of six. Pie charts. A number on every point.

03.2

Data

Qualitative

What people said and felt. The chart shows structure (which themes, how often, how feelings moved), and the quotes stay on the page as evidence.

Use
Clusters for themes, curves for one story. Always cite who said it.

Never
Word clouds. A solid line for something that was not measured.

03.3

Data

Timelines

Time is the route. The study plan is a route with dated stations; the diary week is a set of parallel lanes, one per participant, where every spend is a node at the hour it happened.

03.4

Data

Matrices

Two questions at once. Who showed which pattern; which usability issues to fix first.

03.5

Data

Maps

A map is only drawn when place explains the spending. It is schematic: paper streets, places as stations, and one evening's route in red.

03.6

Data

Networks

Which patterns travel together. Nodes are themes, sized by how many participants showed them; a line joins two themes seen in the same person, thicker when more people showed both.

03.7

Data

Flows

A flow is a route with weight. Line width is the count, so the route itself shows where people went.

03.8

Data

Comparisons

Versions against versions, never participants against each other. Slopes for before and after, diverging bars for agreement.

03.9

Data

Distributions

Show every value. A strip of dots with the median marked says more about six people than a mean and an error bar.

  • AxesA 0.25mm ink hairline. Tick labels blue. No gridlines unless a value must be read off; then paper-deep.
  • MarksBlue by default. One red mark: the one the text is about. Mustard only as a band behind the insight.
  • LabelsDirect labels over legends. Notes in the margin face, joined by a hairline.
  • NeverDual axes, 3D, pies, gradients, averages of six shown without the six.
