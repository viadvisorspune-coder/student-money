01 / The question

Most students in this study knew what the rest of their week held. They had plans, and could name them. Yet at the moment of spending, those plans rarely came up.

This report follows six students through six ordinary days to find out what happens between knowing and spending.

Kharcha is Hindi for spending. The app of the same name was designed and tested across this study.

What happens between knowing and spending?

In every interview, participants could list what the rest of their week held. Five of six named an amount without looking it up.

But when we walked back through each spend, the plan was mentioned at the moment of spending only five times out of twenty-four.

This is not forgetting. The plan was known, and it was not in the room.

What was in the room: who else was there, what the mess had served, whether it was late. Context decided; plans waited.

Eleven of the nineteen were followed by the same sentence, in different words: I'll adjust later.

“Friday was Friday's problem.”

03 · What we sawResearch finding

The mess was bad

Four of six participants ordered food on a night the mess was poor.1 Each described it as unplanned. None of them described it as a choice between that order and something else in their week.

The order was social. In each case at least one friend proposed it, and in three cases the order was split, so the amount on the screen was not the amount paid.2

For P02, the order came on day 3, two days before a planned ₹1,500 birthday dinner. P02 could name the dinner and its cost when asked on day 4.

What these nights share is not the amount, which ranged from ₹170 to ₹350, but the moment: late, hungry, with friends, and with the decision already half-made by the group.

PatternThe decision happens in a social moment, where plans are private and far away.

Kharcha's first versions tried to interrupt this moment with advice. Chapter 06 shows why that failed.

Test finding 04

The suggestion read as an instruction

Five of six read “You might want to…” as the app telling them what to do. Two dismissed the screen without reading the rest.

5 / 6
Design response

Remove the instruction. Keep the context: show what is coming up, not what to do about it.

Test finding 05

The plan cue sat below the fold

Four of six never scrolled to it. Those who did found it useful.

4 / 6
Design response

Move the cue inside the result, next to the figure it affects.

08 / Reflection

What we learned

01

Knowing is not considering

Students know their plans. The work is to bring them into the moment, not to teach them.

02

Advice is heard as judgement

Every version that told students what to do was skipped. Context was read.

03

The decision is social

Spending happens with friends, late, and half-decided. A design has to work in that moment or not at all.
