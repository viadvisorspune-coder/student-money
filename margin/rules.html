06.2

Rules

What not to do

Each pair shows the rule kept (solid line) and broken (dashed line).

Do

Let the route carry the reader: from day to event to quote to finding.

Don't

Draw the route as decoration. If it does not connect two things, remove it.

Do

Contain only what genuinely needs containing, in a flat square paper-deep mount.

Don't

Rounded cards, drop shadows, gradients, glass, rows of three equal boxes. This is not a dashboard.

Do

Colour by type of information: red happens, blue is observed, mustard is meant.

Don't

Give participants colours, or use red and green to judge a spend.

Do

Change the composition from spread to spread: sparse, dense, full-bleed, one quote.

Don't

Change the system: a third typeface, a new colour, a different line weight.

Do

Describe spending in the participants' own terms: what it was for, and when.

Don't

Write “overspent”, “should have”, “too much”, “budget”, “saved ₹…”. No verdicts.

Do

Compare versions of the design, and a participant with their own week.

Don't

Compare participants with each other, or with an average student.

Do

Use hand-drawn marks sparingly: one ring or stroke on the word that matters.

Don't

Scrapbook the page. Tape, stickers and stamps: at most two per spread.

Do

Keep display type for signposts: headlines, days, figures.

Don't

Set a paragraph in capitals, or a headline in italic.

06.3

Rules

Accessibility

  1. ContrastRunning text is blue (5.26 : 1 on paper) or ink. Route red (3.56 : 1) is for labels, notes and folios only, and never the only place a fact appears. Mustard (1.89 : 1) is for display headlines of 34pt and above, and every headline is repeated in the contents.
  2. Never colour aloneNode shape, line style, outcome shape and direct labels carry meaning; colour repeats it. Every chart reads in greyscale.
  3. Minimum sizesBody 8.5pt. Nothing smaller than 6pt in print. Chart labels at least 6pt at final size.
  4. Every chart has its numbersIn print, the values are labelled or tabled on the page. On screen, each chart has a data table and each mark a tooltip.
  5. ImagesEvery image has alt text describing what it shows and why it is there. Placeholders say their format.
  6. Reading orderThe route shows the reading order. Where it cannot, the text order in the file follows the route, not the page position.
  7. MotionScreen only. Respects reduced-motion: the route is drawn and every node visible from the start.
  8. Vertical typeOnly for short labels (a day, a time, a code) and always bottom to top.
06.4

Rules

Data integrity

  1. Always the base“5 of 6”, never “83%”. Every count shows what it is out of.
  2. Show the sixWith a sample this small, draw every participant as a mark. An average is shown only beside the values it summarises, and the median is preferred.
  3. Length starts at zeroBars and areas start at zero. One axis per chart. No 3D, no dual axes.
  4. Say how it was countedEvery figure carries a source line or footnote: method, dates, n, and what was excluded.
  5. Keep counter-evidenceAn insight shows the participant it does not fit, in a margin note on the same spread.
  6. Reported is not measuredFeelings, recollections and estimates are drawn dashed and labelled as reported.
  7. One source of numbersAll figures come from one dataset. The documentation uses js/sample-data.js; replace it with the study data before any page is used, and delete every “sample data” label only then.
  8. Plans are not spendingA plan's amount may be shown as the participant wrote it, but is never added to a spending total.
  9. No comparison between studentsA participant is compared only with their own week, never with another participant or an average.
  10. RoundingRupees to the whole rupee with Indian grouping (₹1,20,000). Times to the minute. Scores to one decimal only where the scale has decimals.

06.6

Rules

Composition

Held constant

Type, colour, line weight, node shapes, spacing unit, image treatment, annotation style, page furniture.

Allowed to move

Asymmetry, lines crossing columns and the fold, oversized numbers, floating objects, full-bleed moments, empty pages.

Per spread

One insight. One mustard. At most three objects, two stickers or stamps, one duotone image. A folio unless the page bleeds.

Across the report

No two consecutive spreads share a layout. Every chapter opens with 05.01 and every test round uses 05.09.
