Margin

Design system  /  Margin research report  /  v0.1

01

A visual language for following money through everyday life. The research is not decorated: a spending day becomes a line, a spend becomes a node, a plan that has not happened yet becomes a ghost node, and a finding becomes a place the line arrives at.

This part sets the foundations: type, colour, grid, spacing, shape, icons, imagery and motion.

foundations

The system is consistent; the compositions are not. Every page may move differently, but it moves with these parts.

01.3

Foundations

Grid

A4 portrait pages, read as spreads. Six columns per page with a 5mm gutter. The grid is there, but the page never announces it: text blocks start on columns, the route snaps to the 2mm unit, and the route is free to cross columns and the fold.

  • MarginsTop 16mm, bottom 18mm, inner 20mm, outer 14mm. Folios sit in the bottom margin, outer corner.
  • SnapText blocks start and end on column edges. Route turns land on multiples of 2mm.
  • Break itThe route, a headline or an artifact may cross the gutter or bleed. Body text never does.
01.4

Foundations

Spacing

One unit of 2mm. Loose compositions still line up because every distance is a step on this scale.

  • Route to textAt least 6mm (space-3) from the line's centre to any text it passes or encloses.
  • Node to label3mm between a node and the station label in its break.
  • Between stopsAt least 24mm (space-12) between two stops on the route, so each reads alone.
01.5

Foundations

Shape

Margin has one shape: the route. It is a thick red line with round ends, every turn rounded, stopping at nodes. It breaks to let a label through, turns dashed when the story is interrupted, and loops around a block of text when it arrives somewhere. The route does the work that cards do elsewhere.

Symbol grammar

  • ContainersIf something must be contained, use a flat, square paper-deep mount. No borders, radius or shadow.
  • CrossingRoutes may cross without a junction mark. A crossing is not a connection; a shared node is.
  • NeverRounded cards, drop shadows, glass effects, or a row of three equal boxes.
01.6

Foundations

Iconography

Not interface icons, but objects: the things a spend was for. They are flat ink silhouettes with a worn print texture and at most one spot colour, placed beside a stop and turned slightly, like the reference's bottle, house and car.

  • Size18mm to 40mm on the page. Turned between −15° and +20°. One object per stop, at most three per spread.
  • ColourInk plus one spot colour at most, used for the part that matters (the chai, the auto's stripe).
  • NeverOutline UI icons, emoji, 3D renders, coins-and-piggy-bank finance clichés.

Foundations

Imagery

Screens and photographs are evidence, so they are treated as artifacts collected during the study: labelled with the version, the participant and the date, and pointed at by a hairline when one detail matters.

Design response

Remove the instruction. Keep the context.

  • ScreensAlways in an ink device frame on paper, labelled with version, participant and date. Never cropped UI floating on its own.
  • PointingA 0.3mm red hairline runs from the observation to the exact element, which gets a 0.5mm red outline.
  • PhotographsBlack and white, high contrast, with grain to match the objects. No faces of participants.

Motion

Only on screen, and only one idea: the route draws itself the first time you see it, and each stop appears as the line reaches it. On paper, and for anyone who prefers reduced motion, everything is already drawn.
