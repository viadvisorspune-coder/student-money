Margin

Information grammar

Nine kinds of statement a research report makes, and how each looks on the page. A reader should be able to tell, before reading a word, whether they are looking at something observed, something claimed, or something the team is unsure of.

The chainEvery insight in the report can be walked back along this route to the evidence it stands on.

02.1

Evidence

Something recorded: a diary entry, a screen recording, a count, a photograph.

  • Always names method, participant and day.
  • Always carries its weight: how many of six.
  • Blue: it was observed.
Evidence 12, Diary, P02, Day 3, 20:15

Food order, ₹280. Remark: “mess was bad”. Split with three friends.

4 / 6 ordered on a poor mess night

.m-callout--blue + .m-kicker + .m-freq

02.2

Claims

A sentence the report asserts, built from evidence.

  • Set as a heading, never as a quote.
  • Lists the evidence it rests on.
  • A solid route when two or more sources agree.

Ordering food is a social decision, not a budgeting one.

Rests on E12, E14, E19

.m-heading + .m-break, the route shows it is supported

02.3

Participants

The people in the study, as codes with only the context that explains their spending.

  • P01–P06. Never a name, a face, or a colour.
  • Context: year, living situation, money in.
  • Consistent code across every chapter.

02.4

Quotes

A participant's own words, verbatim.

  • Italic serif, blue. Never paraphrased inside quote marks.
  • Cited as code, day, method.
  • Cuts shown with […]; added words in [brackets].

“I'll just skip something next week […] it always works out.”

P01, Day 5, Interview

.m-pullquote, .m-bigquote, inline “…” in body

02.5

Observations

What a researcher saw, written at the time, before interpretation.

  • Field-note style: italic, with a hollow marker.
  • Timestamped.
  • Describes; never explains.

Scrolled the result screen twice, stopped on the figure, did not scroll to the plans cue. Put the phone face down.Observation, P05, Test round 2, 14:22

.m-field-note

02.6

Insights

What the evidence means, stated once, and remembered.

  • A yellow card: the route arrives and holds it.
  • One per spread, and the only yellow on it.
  • Always with frequency and confidence.

02.7

Decisions

What the team did because of an insight.

  • Introduced with an arrow, the design consequence.
  • Names the insight it answers.
  • Blue side rule: a response, not a finding.

02.8

Outcomes

What happened when a decision was tested.

  • Before and after, same task, same six people.
  • Outcome by shape: completed, with help, not completed.
  • A changed direction earns a turning point.

02.9

Uncertainty

What the study cannot say, said plainly.

  • Dashed: the route, the loop, the underline.
  • Low confidence shown, not hidden.
  • Open questions are ghost nodes: not yet known.
